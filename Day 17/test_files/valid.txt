The Amazon rainforest is the largest tropical rainforest in the world, covering
over 5.5 million square kilometers across nine countries in South America, with
Brazil containing the majority. It is home to an estimated 390 billion individual
trees divided into 16,000 species, and it produces about 20% of the world's oxygen,
which has earned it the nickname "the lungs of the Earth."

The rainforest plays a critical role in regulating the global climate by absorbing
massive amounts of carbon dioxide. Deforestation, driven largely by cattle ranching,
logging, and agriculture, has reduced the forest's size significantly over the past
50 years. Scientists warn that if deforestation continues past a certain tipping
point, large parts of the rainforest could transition into a drier savanna-like
ecosystem, releasing enormous amounts of stored carbon into the atmosphere.

Indigenous communities have lived in the Amazon for thousands of years and many
depend on the forest directly for food, medicine, and shelter. Conservation efforts
today involve a mix of protected reserves, sustainable agriculture initiatives, and
international agreements aimed at reducing illegal logging and mining.
